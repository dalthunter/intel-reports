Threat Actor Profile — SaaS Data-Theft & Extortion Collective

ShinyHunters

aka ShinyCorp · sp1d3rhunters · UNC6040 (access) / UNC6240 (extortion) · allied as "Scattered Lapsus$ Hunters"

A financially motivated data-theft and extortion collective active since 2020, built on a pay-or-leak playbook that has evolved from smash-and-grab consumer database breaches into the largest sustained OAuth and vishing campaign against Salesforce environments on record — run in close, documented coordination with Scattered Spider.

Section 01

Key Judgments

ShinyHunters is not a fixed roster but a persona-led collective. A leadership figure operating as ShinyCorp (also seen as sp1d3rhunters/shinyc0rp) has directed the brand since at least 2020, took ownership of BreachForums after its founder's 2023 arrest, and publicly disavowed the forum as compromised by imposters once the FBI seized it in October 2025 — group operations continued uninterrupted regardless.

The group's core method has shifted from database exfiltration to identity abuse: since mid-2025 the dominant vector has been vishing victims into authorizing a fraudulent OAuth application impersonating Salesforce's "Data Loader" tool, then riding that trust relationship into downstream SaaS and cloud-warehouse data. No Salesforce vulnerability has been exploited — every documented intrusion abuses legitimate, victim-granted access.

ShinyHunters and Scattered Spider operate as effectively one extended collective as of 2025. FINRA, ReliaQuest, and other trusted-third-party reporting confirm shared infrastructure, overlapping Telegram administration, and joint claims of responsibility (e.g. Jaguar Land Rover), consistent with the group's own "Scattered Lapsus$ Hunters" branding.

The 2025–2026 Salesforce campaign is unprecedented in scale for this actor: public reporting attributes over 1,000 compromised organizations and a claimed 1.5 billion stolen records to the campaign, including an attempted extortion of Salesforce itself. Follow-on token abuse via Salesloft Drift, Gainsight, and Anodot integrations has repeatedly reopened access even after the initial vector was contained.

Law enforcement pressure has been real but not disruptive: Sébastien Raoult's 2022 arrest and 2024 sentencing, four additional French arrests in June 2025, and the FBI's October 2025 BreachForums seizure each produced headlines without a corresponding pause in operations — new campaigns (Anodot in April 2026, the Klue breach in June 2026) followed within weeks each time.

Analyst Note

The throughline across six years of ShinyHunters activity is that the "hack" is almost always a person, not a system. Whether it's a 2020 forum credential dump or a 2026 fraudulent OAuth consent screen, the group's actual skill is convincing someone with legitimate access to hand it over. That's consistent with, and increasingly inseparable from, Scattered Spider's playbook — treat the two as one threat picture going forward.

Section 02

Actor Profile

Origin & structure

ShinyHunters surfaced on dark web forums in May 2020, believed to have formed as early as 2019, and announced itself with a two-week burst of breaches against more than a dozen companies — Tokopedia (91 million records), Microsoft's private GitHub repositories, Wattpad (270 million records), Zoosk, HomeChef, and Minted among them. The group is led by a persona known as ShinyCorp (also referenced as sp1d3rhunters and shinyc0rp across Telegram), though its broader composition is fluid and contested by researchers. ShinyHunters took over administration of BreachForums after founder "Pompompurin's" 2023 arrest; when the FBI seized the forum's infrastructure in October 2025, the group leaked the forum's own 300,000-user database and publicly warned that any surviving BreachForums domains were impostors.

Targeting philosophy

Early ShinyHunters (2020–2021) was opportunistic: breach a consumer platform's database, sell or leak it. By 2024 the group had moved into supply-chain access brokering, most notably the Snowflake campaign — a compromised EPAM Systems employee endpoint yielded unencrypted Snowflake credentials stored in Jira, cascading into roughly 160 organizations including AT&T, Ticketmaster, and Santander. Since mid-2025 the group has concentrated on the Salesforce ecosystem specifically: vishing-driven OAuth consent abuse, followed by token theft from trusted third-party integrations (Salesloft Drift, Gainsight, Anodot) that grants access to every downstream customer of those platforms in one move — a force multiplier well beyond what direct breaches alone would achieve.

Extortion model

ShinyHunters runs a straightforward pay-or-leak model: private ransom demand first, public auction or leak on forums, Telegram, or a branded leak site if refused, with proof samples historically hosted via Limewire and communicated over email or Tox. Pressure tactics extend to DDoS attacks, harassment of personnel, and public deadline threats — the March 2026 Experience Cloud campaign, for instance, threatened mass data release beginning March 14, 2026 unless victims engaged. Reporting from DoControl also notes signs of a nascent "product roadmap" resembling ransomware-as-a-service, suggesting further convergence with traditional RaaS economics.

Analyst Note

Watch the EPAM/Snowflake pattern as a template, not a one-off: ShinyHunters has shown a repeated preference for compromising one well-placed SaaS or integration vendor and then working outward to every downstream customer, rather than attacking targets one at a time. Salesloft Drift, Gainsight, and Anodot are three separate instances of the exact same move in under a year.

Section 03

Timeline of Activity

May 2020

Public debut

A rapid burst of breaches announces the group: Tokopedia (91M records), Microsoft GitHub repositories, Wattpad (270M records), and several smaller consumer platforms within two weeks.

May 2022 / Jan 2024

Raoult arrest & sentencing

French national Sébastien Raoult ("Sezyo Kaizen") is arrested in Morocco, extradited to the US, and sentenced in January 2024 to three years and over $5M in restitution — the group's most significant legal setback to date.

2024

Snowflake supply-chain campaign

A compromised EPAM Systems endpoint exposes unencrypted Snowflake credentials in Jira, cascading into roughly 160 organizations including AT&T (110M records), Ticketmaster (560M records), and Santander (30M records).

Jun 2025

Salesforce vishing campaign begins; French arrests

Google's Threat Intelligence Group begins tracking Salesforce-focused vishing as UNC6040/UNC6240; Google's own corporate Salesforce instance is hit. The same month, French authorities arrest four individuals linked to ShinyHunters/BreachForums administration.

Aug–Sep 2025

Drift token theft & JLR breach

Stolen Salesloft Drift OAuth tokens expose roughly 760 downstream Salesforce customers. In September, Jaguar Land Rover's production shutdown is jointly claimed by ShinyHunters and Scattered Spider; the Instructure/Canvas breach (275M individuals) is also traced to this period.

Oct–Nov 2025

BreachForums seized; Gainsight token abuse

The FBI seizes BreachForums infrastructure; ShinyHunters leaks the forum's own user data and disavows remaining domains. In November, stolen Gainsight OAuth tokens expose 200+ additional Salesforce instances.

Dec 2025

Coinbase extortion attempt

An insider-enabled breach at Coinbase leads to a $20M ransom demand; Coinbase refuses and offers a matching $20M reward for information. A former Coinbase employee is arrested the same month.

Mar–Apr 2026

Experience Cloud campaign & Anodot breach

Automated scanning (the "AuraInspector" tooling) drives a mass extortion campaign against misconfigured Salesforce Experience Cloud portals. In April, a breach of analytics vendor Anodot yields stolen tokens reaching Google BigQuery and Snowflake data for downstream customers including Rockstar Games and Zara.

Jun–Jul 2026

Klue breach & year-long campaign mapped

A disused legacy credential at competitive-intelligence platform Klue is exploited to harvest OAuth tokens reaching customers including Huntress and Recorded Future. In July, Microsoft publishes research formally mapping the mid-2025-to-mid-2026 Salesforce campaign to three distinct OAuth-abuse techniques.

Analyst Note

Notice how little dead time there is between "law enforcement action" and "next major breach" in this timeline. The June 2025 arrests, the October 2025 forum seizure, and the December 2025 Coinbase arrest all sit within weeks of a new named victim. Plan detection and response around the assumption that arrests slow individuals, not the brand.

Section 05

Kill Chain & MITRE ATT&CK Mapping

ShinyHunters' current kill chain barely touches malware. The group operates almost entirely inside legitimate SaaS trust relationships — OAuth grants, API access, and integration tokens — which makes this a harder chain to signature and a more useful one to map behaviorally.

01

Initial Access

T1598.004Spearphishing Voice

Operators call employees impersonating IT support and walk them through authorizing a fraudulent OAuth application — most notably a spoofed "Data Loader" impersonating Salesforce's own tool — via the legitimate device-authorization consent flow.

T1199Trusted Relationship

Rather than attacking targets directly, the group repeatedly compromises a shared SaaS integration vendor (Salesloft Drift, Gainsight, Anodot) and inherits legitimate access to every one of that vendor's downstream customers.

02

Execution & Persistence

T1550.001Use Alternate Authentication Material: Application Access Token

Stolen OAuth refresh tokens are reused directly against Salesforce, Google BigQuery, and Snowflake APIs, persisting access well past the original point of compromise without needing further credential theft.

T1556Modify Authentication Process

In January 2026 campaigns tracked as UNC6661/UNC6671, operators were observed deleting MFA-enrollment notifications in some Okta-linked environments, suppressing the victim's own visibility into a new device being registered.

03

Discovery & Defense Evasion

T1595Active Scanning

Automated tooling (reported under the name "AuraInspector") scans the internet for misconfigured, publicly exposed Salesforce Experience Cloud portals at scale, converting isolated configuration mistakes into a systemic exposure list.

T1078.004Valid Accounts: Cloud Accounts

Because access rides on legitimate OAuth grants and valid API tokens rather than exploited vulnerabilities, activity blends into normal SaaS administrative and integration traffic almost by default.

04

Collection

T1213Data from Information Repositories

Bulk API pulls against Salesforce objects, SharePoint, OneDrive, and connected Gong/BigQuery/Snowflake data stores extract customer records, business contacts, and internal documentation directly through sanctioned application interfaces.

05

Exfiltration

T1567Exfiltration Over Web Service

Data moves out directly over the same legitimate APIs used to access it — no separate malware-based exfiltration channel is typically required, since the stolen token already grants a sanctioned data path.

06

Impact

T1657Financial Theft / Extortion

Standard pay-or-leak pressure follows: private ransom demand, then public deadline threats, DDoS, harassment, and leak-site or Telegram publication if payment is refused — most visibly in the March 2026 Experience Cloud campaign's public leak deadline.

Analyst Note

There's effectively no "malware phase" in this kill chain — from initial access through exfiltration, everything rides on legitimate OAuth tokens and sanctioned API calls. That means EDR and AV coverage contribute almost nothing here. Detection has to live in SaaS-layer telemetry: OAuth grant monitoring, anomalous Bulk API volume, and third-party integration audits.

Section 06

Technical Deep Dive

Primary attack surface

Unlike a traditional ransomware or exploit-driven actor, ShinyHunters' 2025–2026 campaign has no headline CVE — the group's leverage comes from OAuth trust design, not a software flaw. Two adjacent, real vulnerabilities have intersected with the group's activity and are worth tracking separately: a flaw in Instructure Canvas's Free-For-Teacher account program (exploited in the group's May 2026 return to the Instructure breach), and the TanStack npm supply-chain compromise, which yielded a GitHub token later used against Grafana. Neither is a Salesforce vulnerability; both illustrate the same underlying pattern of riding a trusted third-party weakness into a much larger blast radius.

Tooling & named artifacts observed

Fraudulent "Data Loader" OAuth app — vishing-delivered, spoofs Salesforce's own tool AuraInspector — automated Experience Cloud misconfiguration scanner Stolen Drift / Gainsight / Anodot OAuth tokens — third-party integration abuse Device code phishing — core delivery method for the fraudulent app grant Telegram ("Sim Land" channel) — coordination with SIM-swap ecosystem Tox / email / Limewire-hosted samples — extortion communication & proof BreachForums (legacy) — data sale/leak platform, seized Oct 2025

A note on the Scattered Spider relationship

Multiple trusted sources — FINRA, ReliaQuest, and Microsoft among them — now treat ShinyHunters and Scattered Spider as closely intertwined rather than merely "linked." ReliaQuest identified overlapping typosquatted Salesforce phishing domain patterns between the two brands, and ShinyCorp's own August 2025 move into administering the "Sim Land" Telegram channel points to direct coordination with SIM-swapping infrastructure historically associated with Scattered Spider's earliest campaigns. For defenders, the practical implication is that mitigations effective against one collective's help-desk and identity-abuse tradecraft are very likely to transfer to the other.

Analyst Note

I've kept the CVE table short deliberately — there's almost nothing to patch here. If your org has any Salesforce, Drift, Gainsight, or similar SaaS integration footprint, the actionable technical surface is OAuth app governance and API monitoring, not a vulnerability scan.

Section 08

Detection & Mitigation Priorities

Ordered by estimated impact-to-effort ratio for a mid-sized enterprise defending against this actor's observed playbook.

  • P1Require admin approval for new Connected App / OAuth grants in Salesforce and verify any "Data Loader"-named integration is the genuine Salesforce-published app. This directly closes the group's dominant 2025–2026 access vector.
  • P1Harden help-desk identity verification against vishing, matching the same call-back/attestation controls warranted against Scattered Spider — the two groups share this exact technique.
  • P1Enable Salesforce Shield / Real-Time Event Monitoring to catch anomalous Bulk API activity and API-token usage patterns that Salesforce's standard audit logs miss.
  • P2Inventory and regularly rotate tokens for every third-party SaaS integration (Drift, Gainsight, Anodot, and similar), and deprovision disused legacy integrations — the Klue breach began with exactly this kind of forgotten credential.
  • P2Alert on suppressed or missing MFA-enrollment notifications, particularly in Okta-linked environments, as an indicator of the group's device-registration evasion technique.
  • P2Audit public-facing Salesforce Experience Cloud portals for guest-access misconfigurations before automated scanning tools like the group's reported "AuraInspector" find them first.
  • P3Monitor for domain registrations impersonating your Salesforce/SSO branding, particularly the "-my-salesforce" and "keyword-salesforce" naming patterns identified in public reporting.
  • P3Prepare an extortion response playbook (legal, comms, and law enforcement contacts) in advance, given the group's consistent use of public deadline threats and harassment pressure.
Analyst Note

If you can only act on three items here: lock down OAuth app approval in Salesforce, harden help-desk verification, and inventory your third-party SaaS integration tokens. Every named 2025–2026 breach in this report traces back to one of those three gaps.
