Threat Actor Profile: Data Extortion & Leak Marketplace Collective

ShinyHunters

aka ShinyCorp · UNC6040 · UNC6041 · UNC6395 · UNC6661 · UNC6671

A financially motivated cybercriminal collective active since 2020, known for mass database theft, ownership of the BreachForums leak marketplace, and a pivot toward identity-centric SaaS and cloud extortion in partnership with adjacent "Com" affiliated crews.

Section 01

Actor Profile

Origin & structure

ShinyHunters emerged around 2020 as a data theft and resale persona, initially built around dumping stolen databases on underground forums. The group later took ownership of BreachForums, the successor to RaidForums, and has operated it through several relaunches. Rather than a fixed roster, ShinyHunters functions as a brand and persona shared across a loose network, overlapping heavily with other "Com" linked crews such as Scattered Spider and Lapsus$, who reportedly began coordinating on joint extortion campaigns.

Targeting philosophy

Early activity centered on opportunistic theft from exposed cloud storage and source code repositories. Since 2024, the group has shifted toward deliberate, identity driven intrusions against large SaaS and cloud platforms, most notably Snowflake customer environments and the Salesforce ecosystem, sweeping up victims across retail, luxury goods, airlines, education, and technology in large connected campaigns.

Extortion model

Data theft without encryption has historically been the group's signature: victims are pressured through email, Tox chat, and a ShinyHunters branded leak site rather than a ransomware note. In 2025 the group began experimenting with a ransomware-as-a-service offering under the alias ShinySp1d3r, positioning itself to fill the gap left by other RaaS disruptions, while continuing its core business of bulk data theft and resale.

Timeline of Activity

2020

Public emergence

The ShinyHunters persona surfaces selling stolen databases from a string of e-commerce and tech companies on underground forums.

May 2022

Raoult arrest

French programmer Sébastien Raoult, tied to the group, is arrested in Morocco and later extradited to the US.

Jun 2023

BreachForums v2

ShinyHunters partners with Baphomet to relaunch BreachForums after its original founder's arrest.

Apr to Jun 2024

Snowflake customer campaign

Stolen credentials against unprotected Snowflake customer accounts lead to mass data theft and extortion across dozens of organizations.

2025

Salesforce & SaaS pivot

Vishing campaigns, tracked as UNC6040, and OAuth token abuse against Salesforce, Drift, and Gainsight integrations hit Google, Adidas, Qantas, Allianz Life, and multiple luxury brands.

Jun 2025

France arrests

French authorities arrest four suspects tied to the ShinyHunters, Hollow, Noct, and Depressed personas over BreachForums administration.

Oct 2025

BreachForums seized

The FBI seizes BreachForums infrastructure; ShinyHunters publicly walks away from the forum and leaks data on 300,000 of its own users.

2026

Education & SaaS extortion wave

Campaigns tracked as UNC6661 and UNC6671 hit universities, and reported victims through the year include Wynn Resorts, Panera Bread, Grubhub, Canvas, and 7-Eleven.

Section 03

Kill Chain & MITRE ATT&CK Mapping

ShinyHunters rarely needs to exploit software. Access begins with a phone call, a stolen OAuth token, or an exposed credential, and the goal from there is bulk data collection rather than deployment of a payload.

01

Initial Access

T1566.004Spearphishing Voice (Vishing)

Callers pose as internal IT support and walk an employee through authorizing a lookalike connected app or entering credentials into a fake verification page.

T1078Valid Accounts

Credentials stolen through infostealer logs or purchased from initial access brokers are reused to log directly into cloud and SaaS platforms.

T1190Exploit Public-Facing Application

Misconfigured cloud storage buckets and exposed developer secrets provide a secondary, more opportunistic entry point.

02

Persistence & Privilege Access

T1528Steal Application Access Token

OAuth and refresh tokens tied to third party SaaS integrations, such as Drift and Gainsight, are abused to reach connected Salesforce environments without triggering a login alert.

T1556.003Modify Authentication Process: Web Session Cookie

Stolen session cookies and forged authentication material allow continued access after an initial credential is rotated.

03

Discovery & Defense Evasion

T1087Account Discovery

Compromised access is used to enumerate connected SaaS tenants, cloud storage buckets, and downstream integrations before bulk collection begins.

MFA Notification Suppression

In some intrusions, a Gmail add-on has reportedly been silently authorized to delete MFA enrollment notification emails, delaying victim detection of a new device registration.

04

Collection & Exfiltration

T1530Data from Cloud Storage

Large scale queries pull records directly from Snowflake warehouses, Google BigQuery, SharePoint, OneDrive, and Salesforce objects ahead of extortion.

Bulk Export via Legitimate Tooling

Native export and API functionality of the compromised SaaS platform itself is used to move data out, blending with normal administrative activity.

05

Impact

Data Leak Site & Direct Extortion

Victims are pressured through email, Tox chat, and a branded leak site with sample records; some campaigns add harassment or DDoS pressure, and a smaller share involve an affiliate ransomware payload under the ShinySp1d3r brand.

Section 05

Detection & Mitigation Priorities

Ordered by impact-to-effort ratio for a mid-sized enterprise running SaaS and cloud data platforms.

  • P1Audit and restrict OAuth/connected app scopes in Salesforce, Google Workspace, and Microsoft 365, especially anything with mail or bulk data export permissions.
  • P1Require callback verification through a known number before any help desk password reset or MFA re-enrollment.
  • P1Enforce IP allowlisting and least-privilege API access on Snowflake, BigQuery, and other analytics warehouses holding customer data.
  • P2Alert on new MFA device registrations and on deletion of MFA enrollment emails before a user reports missing notifications.
  • P2Move to phishing resistant MFA (FIDO2 hardware keys) for accounts with SaaS admin or data export rights.
  • P3Monitor underground forums and leak sites for early mention of your organization ahead of a direct extortion contact.
Analyst Note

ShinyHunters is best understood as a persona and a marketplace as much as a fixed group: it began by dumping stolen databases for sale, grew into running BreachForums itself, and has since moved toward calling employees or abusing app integrations to pull data straight out of platforms like Salesforce and Snowflake. Organizations should pay attention because the group rarely touches malware at all, which means antivirus and endpoint tools see very little; the real exposure sits in which connected apps and integrations have been trusted with broad data access. This matters because law enforcement has repeatedly arrested individuals and seized infrastructure tied to the group, yet new campaigns keep surfacing under the same brand, since the underlying access methods and the wider network behind them remain intact.
