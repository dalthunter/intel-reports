Threat Actor Profile: State-Sponsored Espionage & Crypto Theft Program

Lazarus Group

Section 01

Actor Profile

Origin & structure

Lazarus Group is attributed to North Korea's Reconnaissance General Bureau (RGB) and has been active since at least 2009. "Lazarus Group" functions as both a broad umbrella term and a label for narrower, related clusters; names like BlueNoroff, Andariel, TraderTraitor, and APT38 are sometimes used interchangeably in public reporting and sometimes track more specific sub-clusters, so attribution should be read carefully against its source and date rather than treated as one uniform actor.

Dual mission

Unlike most state-sponsored APTs, Lazarus operates with a dual mandate: traditional espionage and disruptive attacks alongside large-scale financially motivated cybercrime. The group's public record spans the 2014 Sony Pictures destructive attack, the attempted Bangladesh Bank SWIFT heist, the 2017 WannaCry ransomware outbreak, and, increasingly, cryptocurrency theft used as a sanctions-evasion mechanism to fund the regime, including its weapons programs.

Targeting philosophy

Cryptocurrency exchanges and DeFi platforms are the current center of gravity, targeted for gaps in cold wallet security or upstream software dependencies. A parallel, long-running track targets software developers and IT job seekers directly through fake recruiting processes, aimed at both credential and cryptocurrency theft and at establishing illicit North Korean remote-worker access to Western companies. Defense, aerospace, and South Korean financial, semiconductor, and telecom sectors remain steady secondary targets for espionage.

Section 02

Timeline of Activity

2009

Earliest known activity

Initial operations begin, later tied together retrospectively as the group's activity is tracked over the following decade.

2014

Sony Pictures attack

A destructive intrusion against Sony Pictures Entertainment, tied to the film "The Interview," brings the group its first major public attribution as Guardians of Peace.

2016 to 2017

Bangladesh Bank & WannaCry

An attempted SWIFT transfer heist against Bangladesh Bank and the global WannaCry ransomware outbreak both get tied to the group, expanding its reputation into financial crime and disruptive attack.

2020 to 2023

ThreatNeedle & Operation Dream Job

Watering-hole and fake job-offer campaigns target defense, aerospace, and cryptocurrency sector employees across multiple countries.

2024

Contagious Interview matures

Fake developer job interviews trick candidates into running malicious npm packages, deploying the BeaverTail stealer and InvisibleFerret backdoor at scale.

Feb 2025

Bybit heist

Roughly $1.46 to 1.5 billion in Ethereum is stolen from exchange Bybit by compromising Safe{Wallet} multi-signature infrastructure and manipulating the transaction approval process; the FBI publicly attributes the theft to Lazarus on February 26, 2025.

2025

Record theft year

DPRK-linked actors are estimated to have stolen $2.02 billion in cryptocurrency across the year, a 51 percent increase over the prior year.

Apr 2026

KelpDAO exploit & Zerion incident

A $292 million exploit of KelpDAO is attributed to the same TraderTraitor cluster, alongside a separate incident at Zerion using AI-enabled social engineering to compromise team sessions, credentials, and private keys.

Section 03

Kill Chain & MITRE ATT&CK Mapping

Lazarus runs two parallel playbooks that converge on the same goal: long-term access followed by asset extraction, whether the objective is intelligence or cryptocurrency.

01

Initial Access

Contagious Interview (Fake Job Offers)

Fake recruiters lure software developers through a staged interview process, ultimately convincing the candidate to run a malicious npm package or take-home coding "test" as part of the hiring exercise.

T1195Supply Chain Compromise

Trusted vendor and upstream software dependencies, such as multi-signature wallet infrastructure, are compromised to reach a downstream high-value target rather than attacking it directly.

Spearphishing & AI-Enabled Social Engineering

Highly targeted spearphishing, increasingly augmented with AI-generated deepfakes and more convincing pretexting, is used to compromise individual employees' sessions, credentials, and private keys.

02

Execution

BeaverTail

A JavaScript-based credential and cryptocurrency wallet stealer delivered through malicious packages as part of the Contagious Interview campaign.

InvisibleFerret

A Python backdoor providing persistent access and data exfiltration capability, typically deployed alongside BeaverTail once initial access is established.

03

Defense Evasion

Living off the Land & Deep Obfuscation

Legitimate tools such as PowerShell are used alongside heavy code obfuscation, including "Matryoshka"-style nested obfuscation, to evade signature-based security tooling.

T1071Encrypted Command and Control Channels

Command and control traffic is layered with additional encryption inside an already-encrypted SSL tunnel, complicating network-based detection.

04

Persistence

Scheduled Tasks & Service Creation

Long-term access is maintained through standard persistence mechanisms rather than exotic techniques, prioritizing durability and low visibility over cleverness.

05

Impact

Transaction Manipulation & Cryptocurrency Extraction

Once positioned inside exchange or wallet infrastructure, the group manipulates the transaction approval process so legitimate signers unknowingly authorize a malicious transfer, then rapidly launders proceeds through mixing services and chain-hopping to evade tracing.

Section 05

Detection & Mitigation Priorities

Ordered by impact-to-effort ratio, with distinct guidance for software organizations and cryptocurrency platforms.

  • P1Sandbox and vet any code from an external interview or hiring exercise before running it on a developer's primary machine.
  • P1Require independent, multi-party verification for high-value transaction approvals, especially for multi-signature wallets and exchange cold-to-hot wallet transfers.
  • P1Audit third-party and upstream software dependencies used in wallet and custody infrastructure for supply chain compromise.
  • P2Train staff to recognize AI-enabled social engineering, including deepfake voice or video used in recruiting or vendor impersonation.
  • P2Monitor for known malware families (BeaverTail, InvisibleFerret, ThreatNeedle) using current threat intelligence feeds.
  • P3Screen against OFAC and DPRK-linked wallet designations before processing large or first-time withdrawals.
Analyst Note

Unlike most state sponsored actors, this group uses cybercrime as a major source of national revenue, with cryptocurrency theft linked to North Korea's weapons programs. It targets exchanges, software vendors, and developers, and has remained highly active despite years of sanctions and law enforcement, making it a persistent and evolving threat. Organizations can reduce their risk by enforcing multi factor authentication, securing software development environments, and monitoring for suspicious cryptocurrency related activity.
