Threat Actor Profile: State-Sponsored Pre-Positioning in Critical Infrastructure

Volt Typhoon

aka Vanguard Panda · BRONZE SILHOUETTE · Insidious Taurus · Voltzite · UNC3236 · DEV-0391

A PRC state-sponsored actor, assessed as tied to China's military or intelligence services, active since at least 2021, that avoids malware almost entirely in favor of living-off-the-land techniques, seeking long-term, low-visibility access inside US critical infrastructure ahead of a possible future crisis.

Analyst Note

Volt Typhoon typically focuses on getting into critical infrastructure networks and staying there quietly rather than immediately causing damage. The attackers have used compromised routers, valid accounts, and other internet-facing devices to gain access. Once inside, they often use legitimate Windows tools such as PowerShell, WMI, and Windows Management Instrumentation to move through the network and gather information while trying to avoid detection. They are also known for using “living-off-the-land” techniques, meaning they use tools already installed on the victim's systems instead of relying heavily on custom malware.

Their goal can be to understand the network, maintain access, and position themselves for possible disruption later. I would look for unusual use of PowerShell and WMI, compromised administrator accounts, suspicious activity on network devices, unusual connections between internal systems, command-line activity, and other living-off-the-land behavior.

Section 01

Actor Profile

Origin & attribution

Volt Typhoon is assessed by the NSA, CISA, FBI, and Five Eyes partners to be a People's Republic of China state-sponsored actor, likely affiliated with the PLA or Ministry of State Security. Early activity, dating to at least 2021, targeted military networks, utilities, and maritime ports; the group was formally identified and publicly linked to a 2023 intrusion into telecommunications infrastructure on Guam, a US territory of significant military importance.

Targeting philosophy

Unlike most financially motivated actors profiled elsewhere in this series, Volt Typhoon is not after money. Confirmed targets span communications, energy, transportation, and water and wastewater systems, chosen for their strategic value rather than their payout potential. US officials, including former FBI Director Christopher Wray, have described the group's objective as "pre-positioning" for disruptive or destructive effect during a future geopolitical crisis or conflict, not immediate espionage payoff.

Operational model

The group's defining trait is a near-total avoidance of custom malware in favor of living-off-the-land (LOTL) techniques: native operating system tools, valid stolen credentials, and hands-on-keyboard activity that blend into normal administrator behavior and evade traditional signature-based detection. Command and control traffic is frequently proxied through compromised small office/home office (SOHO) routers and edge devices, making the true source of activity difficult to trace.

Section 02

Timeline of Activity

2021

Earliest known activity

Initial intrusions target military networks and utilities, though the activity is not yet publicly tied to a named actor.

May 2023

Public exposure

Microsoft formally identifies Volt Typhoon following its compromise of Guam telecommunications infrastructure; NSA, FBI, and CISA support the attribution and issue a joint advisory on the group's living-off-the-land tradecraft.

Jan 2024

KV Botnet disrupted

The FBI, under a court order obtained in December 2023, remotely disables Volt Typhoon's implants across compromised end-of-life Cisco and NETGEAR SOHO routers used to proxy the group's traffic.

Feb 2024

CISA/NSA joint advisory (AA24-038A)

Authorities publish detailed findings confirming persistent access to critical infrastructure across multiple US sectors, including a compromised environment in Guam.

Mar to Nov 2024

Botnet rebuild & further disruption

Volt Typhoon rebuilds the KV Botnet within months of the takedown, exploits a zero-day in August, and is met with a second court-authorized FBI disruption in November.

Jun 2024

SingTel breach, Singapore

A breach of Singapore-based telecom provider SingTel is assessed as a likely test run for techniques later intended for use against US targets.

Apr 2026

Expanded edge-device advisory (AA26-113A)

CISA and the UK NCSC jointly describe an expanded "China-nexus covert network" of compromised SOHO routers, VPN appliances, and edge devices supporting continued footholds across utilities and communications providers.

May 2026

CI Fortify launched

CISA launches an initiative aimed at helping critical infrastructure operators maintain offline operational capability, on the assumption that external connectivity may be compromised or severed during a future conflict-tier cyber event.

Section 03

Kill Chain & MITRE ATT&CK Mapping

Volt Typhoon's intrusions are built to look like routine administrator activity. There is rarely a payload to catch; the work is almost entirely hands-on-keyboard, using tools already present on the system.

01

Reconnaissance

Search Open Technical Databases

Operators use internet asset search engines, including the Chinese-developed platform FOFA, to identify already exposed infrastructure ahead of an intrusion attempt.

02

Initial Access

T1190Exploit Public-Facing Application

Known vulnerabilities in edge devices from Fortinet, Ivanti Connect Secure, NETGEAR, Citrix, and Cisco provide the group's primary entry point.

T1133External Remote Services

Legitimate VPN and remote access services are abused once credentials are obtained, blending initial access into expected remote connectivity patterns.

03

Persistence & Command and Control

KV Botnet / SOHO Router Proxying

Compromised end-of-life small office/home office routers and edge devices, including from Cisco, NETGEAR, and DrayTek, are chained together to proxy command and control traffic and obscure the true source of activity.

T1078Valid Accounts

Stolen legitimate credentials, rather than backdoor malware, are the group's primary means of maintaining access across a target environment.

04

Discovery & Lateral Movement

T1057Living off the Land Binaries (LOLBins)

Native command line tools and legitimate but outdated administrative utilities are used for discovery and lateral movement; observed command history shows operators repeating and adjusting commands in an exploratory, hands-on manner rather than running a scripted tool.

Credential Dumping via Legitimate DLLs

In documented intrusions, an outdated version of the legitimate Windows comsvcs.dll has been used with MiniDump against the LSASS process to harvest credentials without deploying separate malware.

05

Collection & Impact

Long-Term Pre-Positioning

Rather than an immediate collection or destructive objective, the assessed goal is durable, low-visibility access that could be leveraged to disrupt critical services during a future crisis or conflict.

Section 05

Detection & Mitigation Priorities

Drawn from CISA, NSA, and FBI joint guidance; ordered by impact-to-effort ratio for a critical infrastructure operator.

  • P1Patch and, where possible, retire end-of-life edge devices (routers, VPN appliances, firewalls) that no longer receive vendor security updates.
  • P1Enforce phishing-resistant MFA on all remote access and administrative accounts, since the group relies on valid credentials rather than malware.
  • P1Baseline and monitor for anomalous LOLBin usage, comparing command line activity against expected administrative behavior rather than relying on file-based signatures.
  • P2Segment IT from OT networks and isolate externally facing services to limit lateral movement toward operational systems.
  • P2Centralize and extend log retention for authentication, PowerShell, and network flow data, since LOTL activity is easy to lose in default, short-retention logging.
  • P3Build offline, manual fallback procedures for critical operational functions, on the assumption that external connectivity could be compromised or severed during a crisis.
