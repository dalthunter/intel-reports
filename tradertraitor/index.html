Threat Actor Profile: DPRK Cryptocurrency Theft Specialist Cluster

TraderTraitor

aka UNC4899 · Jade Sleet · Slow Pisces · PUKCHONG

A North Korean state-sponsored cluster within the Lazarus Group ecosystem, assessed as tied to the Reconnaissance General Bureau, active since at least 2020 and specialized almost exclusively in cryptocurrency and blockchain sector theft, responsible for several of the largest crypto heists on record.

Analyst Note

TraderTraitor typically targets cryptocurrency organizations and individuals, using social engineering to gain access to accounts, wallets, and other sensitive systems. The attackers have used fake job opportunities and other convincing messages to trick targets into communicating with them and sharing information or downloading malicious software. Once they gain access, they can steal credentials, access cryptocurrency wallets, and move stolen funds through different cryptocurrency addresses.

Their attacks focus heavily on gaining access and then using that access to steal cryptocurrency. I would look for suspicious job-related messages, unexpected requests to download files or applications, unusual login activity, stolen credentials, unauthorized wallet activity, cryptocurrency transfers to unfamiliar addresses, and connections to known TraderTraitor infrastructure.

Section 01

Actor Profile

Origin & attribution

TraderTraitor is a designation first used by the FBI, CISA, and US Treasury in an April 2022 joint advisory to describe DPRK-backed activity tied to Lazarus Group, APT38, BlueNoroff, and Stardust Chollima. Different vendors track overlapping activity under their own names: Google/Mandiant as UNC4899, Microsoft as Jade Sleet, and Palo Alto Networks' Unit 42 as Slow Pisces, alongside the industry name PUKCHONG. The cluster is assessed as affiliated with North Korea's Reconnaissance General Bureau and operates as a specialized, financially motivated subset of the broader Lazarus ecosystem.

Targeting philosophy

Unlike the wider Lazarus umbrella's mix of espionage and disruption, TraderTraitor is almost entirely focused on cryptocurrency exchanges, blockchain developers, DeFi protocols, and adjacent cloud and DevOps infrastructure. The group has consistently gone after the people and pipelines behind crypto platforms, developers, engineers, and cloud administrators, rather than attacking exchange front doors directly.

Operational model

Revenue generation for a sanctioned state is the explicit driver, with proceeds assessed to help fund North Korea's weapons programs. The group is defined by patient, well-resourced operations: trojanized developer tooling, fake recruiting pipelines, and, increasingly, quiet persistence inside cloud and CI/CD environments using legitimate-looking configuration changes rather than obvious malware.

Section 02

Timeline of Activity

2020

Cluster activity begins

Google and Mandiant assess the group's activity, distinguishable from broader Lazarus operations, as active from this point, distributing trojanized cryptocurrency trading applications built on Node.js and Electron.

Mar 2022

Ronin Network heist

A fake LinkedIn recruiter convinces an Axie Infinity engineer to download a malicious job offer PDF; the resulting access is used to alter validator nodes and approve roughly $625 million in fraudulent withdrawals.

Apr 2022

US government names TraderTraitor

The FBI, CISA, and Treasury issue a joint advisory formally attributing the trojanized crypto app campaign to DPRK-backed actors under the TraderTraitor designation.

Jun to Jul 2023

JumpCloud & npm supply chain campaigns

UNC4899 compromises cloud identity provider JumpCloud via spearphishing and pushes a malicious update to a handful of cryptocurrency customers; a parallel npm campaign leads GitHub to suspend malicious Jade Sleet-linked accounts.

Mar 2024

DMM Bitcoin breach

A fake recruiter pretext again leads to a compromised developer running a malicious Python test script, contributing to a $308 million theft from Japanese exchange DMM Bitcoin in May 2024.

Feb 2025

Bybit heist

Approximately $1.5 billion in Ethereum is stolen from Bybit via compromised Safe{Wallet} infrastructure and manipulated transaction approvals; the FBI publicly attributes the theft to TraderTraitor.

2025

Cloud-native intrusion, AirDrop delivery

UNC4899 breaches a cryptocurrency firm after a developer AirDrops a trojanized file from a personal to a corporate device, pivoting into Kubernetes and CI/CD infrastructure using living-off-the-cloud persistence techniques.

Apr 2026

KelpDAO / LayerZero exploit

A roughly $292 million theft from cross-chain verification infrastructure is attributed to TraderTraitor / UNC4899 with high confidence, with proceeds laundered through THORChain, Wasabi, Tornado Cash, and Umbra.

Section 03

Kill Chain & MITRE ATT&CK Mapping

Nearly every major TraderTraitor heist traces back to one person: a developer or engineer convinced, through a fake job offer or trusted file transfer, to run something they shouldn't have.

01

Initial Access

Fake Recruiter / Job Offer Lure

Operators pose as recruiters on LinkedIn offering lucrative engineering roles, ultimately convincing the target to download a malicious job offer document or host a "take-home" coding test.

T1195.002Supply Chain Compromise: Compromise Software Supply Chain

Cloud identity and DevOps vendors, such as JumpCloud, have been compromised and used to push malicious updates onward to a small, deliberately chosen set of cryptocurrency industry customers.

Personal-to-Corporate Device Transfer Abuse

Social engineering has been used to get a trojanized file moved from a developer's personal device to their corporate workstation via peer-to-peer transfer tools such as AirDrop, sidestepping corporate email and web filtering entirely.

02

Execution

Trojanized Cryptocurrency Applications

Malicious trading or price-prediction applications, built cross-platform in JavaScript using Node.js and Electron, serve as an early and continuing delivery mechanism.

RN Loader / RN Stealer

A memory-resident Python loader and companion infostealer harvest credentials, cloud configuration, SSH keys, macOS keychain data, browser data, and environment variables, deleting themselves after execution to avoid leaving artifacts on disk.

Malicious Docker Containers

Targets have been convinced, under the guise of freelance development work, to execute malicious Docker containers on their workstations as part of the onboarding process.

03

Persistence & Privilege Escalation

Living Off the Cloud (LOTC)

Kubernetes deployment configurations are altered to automatically execute a bash command whenever new pods are created, establishing persistence through legitimate-looking infrastructure-as-code rather than a standalone backdoor.

CI/CD Service Account Token Theft

Kubernetes resources tied to a victim's CI/CD platform are modified to expose privileged service account tokens in logs, which are then used to escalate privileges and move laterally.

04

Lateral Movement & Collection

Cloud SQL & Container Escape

Legitimate DevOps workflows are abused to harvest further credentials, escape container restrictions, and manipulate cloud database services in pursuit of the assets or approval mechanisms needed for theft.

05

Impact

Fraudulent Transaction Approval & Laundering

Compromised validator, wallet, or cross-chain verification infrastructure is used to approve fraudulent withdrawals, with proceeds rapidly moved through mixing and cross-chain services to complicate tracing and recovery.

Section 05

Detection & Mitigation Priorities

Ordered by impact-to-effort ratio, weighted toward cryptocurrency, blockchain, and cloud-native organizations.

  • P1Treat unsolicited recruiting contact and take-home coding tests as a social engineering vector; require code review and sandboxing before anything is run outside a controlled environment.
  • P1Restrict personal-to-corporate file transfer paths (AirDrop and similar) on devices with access to sensitive infrastructure.
  • P1Enforce least-privilege IAM and rotate CI/CD service account credentials regularly, and alert on tokens appearing in plaintext logs.
  • P2Monitor Kubernetes deployment configuration changes for unauthorized modifications, especially those introducing command execution on pod creation.
  • P2Require independent, multi-party verification for validator and wallet transaction approvals, particularly for cross-chain and bridge infrastructure.
  • P3Enable comprehensive cloud logging (AWS/GCP audit logs, Kubernetes audit logs) and retain it long enough to reconstruct a patient, multi-stage intrusion.
