Threat Actor Profile: Cybercrime Enterprise, POS Theft to Ransomware

FIN7

aka Carbon Spider · GOLD NIAGARA · ITG14 · Sangria Tempest

A financially motivated, Eastern Europe and Russia centered cybercrime group active since roughly 2013, structured like a legitimate business with dedicated malware development, phishing, and money laundering teams, that has evolved from payment card theft into ransomware affiliate operations while surviving repeated indictments and arrests.

Section 01

Actor Profile

Origin & structure

FIN7 has operated since at least 2013, centered in Eastern Europe and Russia, and is organized less like a hacking crew and more like a company: compartmentalized teams handle malware development, phishing operations, money laundering, and management separately from one another. This structure is part of why the group has kept functioning through multiple US indictments and member arrests since 2018, since no single arrest removes the infrastructure or the playbook.

Targeting philosophy

Early campaigns focused heavily on point-of-sale systems in the restaurant, hospitality, and gaming sectors, using fraudulent tech-support phone calls and phishing emails to plant Carbanak based malware and harvest payment card data at scale. Since approximately 2020 the group has pivoted toward big game hunting: high-value, targeted network intrusions across retail, financial services, healthcare, software, and manufacturing, more recently including a documented campaign against a major US automotive manufacturer.

Extortion model

Card data resale was the original revenue engine; since around 2020 the group has increasingly deployed or enabled ransomware, with researchers linking probable FIN7 activity to REvil, DarkSide, BlackMatter, ALPHV, Black Basta, Maze, and Ryuk deployments, alongside its own reported ties to the destructive Anubis ransomware-as-a-service brand. FIN7 has also sold EDR evasion tooling to other criminal groups, monetizing its technical capability even where it isn't the one holding the ransom note.

Section 02

Timeline of Activity

2013

Emergence

The group begins operations centered on payment card theft from point-of-sale systems.

2017 to 2018

Mass retail & restaurant breaches

Extensive POS data theft campaigns hit major food and retail chains across thousands of locations nationwide.

Aug 2018

DOJ indictments

Three Ukrainian men, Dmytro Fedorov, Fedir Hladyr, and Andrii Kolpakov, are indicted over operations against more than 100 companies in 47 US states.

~2020

Pivot to ransomware

The group shifts from opportunistic card theft toward high-value, targeted intrusions and ransomware affiliate activity.

Jan 2022

BadUSB campaign

The FBI warns that FIN7 is mailing malicious USB drives to US companies to install ransomware after being plugged in.

2022 to 2024

AvNeutralizer / EDR evasion tooling

SentinelOne tracks the group's AvNeutralizer (AuKill) tool being sold to and reused by other ransomware operators, including observed ties to Black Basta activity.

Apr 2024

US automotive industry campaign

A large US automotive manufacturer is targeted using a fake IP scanning tool as the initial malware lure.

Dec 2024

Anubis ransomware activity begins

The Anubis ransomware-as-a-service brand, later reported with FIN7-linked distribution infrastructure, becomes active, hitting healthcare, hospitality, and construction victims.

Jun 2025

GrayAlpha infrastructure exposed

Recorded Future's Insikt Group details FIN7-linked GrayAlpha infrastructure using fake 7-Zip sites, fake browser updates, and a traffic distribution system to deliver NetSupport RAT.

2026

Continued activity

FIN7 remains active despite past claims of the group's dissolution, with malicious ad and MSIX installer campaigns continuing to deliver NetSupport RAT.

Section 03

Kill Chain & MITRE ATT&CK Mapping

FIN7's tradecraft spans over a decade of adaptation, from fraudulent tech-support calls to fake software download pages, but the throughline is disciplined initial access followed by rapid, well-tooled escalation.

01

Initial Access

T1566Phishing & Malvertising

Malicious Google ads spoofing legitimate brands and fake software download pages, including impersonated 7-Zip and browser update sites, lure victims into running an MSIX installer or dropper.

T1091Replication Through Removable Media

Malicious USB drives mailed directly to target organizations, styled as gifts or promotional items, execute malware when plugged into a workstation.

Trojanized Legitimate Tooling

Fake IP scanning tools and other seemingly legitimate utilities have been used as an initial malware lure against high-value targets.

02

Execution & Delivery

Custom & Adapted Loaders

Purpose-built loaders including PowerNet and MaskBat decompress and execute the group's payloads, with some variants checking whether the host is enterprise-domain joined before continuing.

Anubis Backdoor

A Python-based backdoor giving full system control via in-memory execution, communicating with command-and-control infrastructure using Base64-encoded data.

03

Defense Evasion

AvNeutralizer (AuKill)

A dedicated EDR impairment tool built and sold by the group, used to disable endpoint security products and shared across multiple ransomware operators.

Shim Database Persistence

Application compatibility shim databases have been abused as a low-visibility persistence mechanism on compromised hosts.

04

Command & Control

NetSupport RAT

A commercially available remote administration tool repurposed as the group's primary remote access payload across its recent malvertising and fake-download campaigns.

05

Impact

Payment Card Theft / Ransomware Deployment

Depending on the campaign, impact ranges from bulk payment card data harvesting from POS environments to full ransomware deployment, with some affiliated strains including a destructive wipe mode that erases files even after ransom payment.

Section 05

Detection & Mitigation Priorities

Ordered by impact-to-effort ratio for a mid-sized enterprise, with attention to the group's diversified initial access playbook.

  • P1Restrict software installation to approved sources and block execution of MSIX installers from outside managed channels.
  • P1Alert on EDR/AV service tampering or unexpected crashes, a leading indicator of active defense evasion tooling.
  • P1Block or closely control removable media on workstations, particularly unsolicited or unrecognized devices.
  • P2Filter malicious advertising and enforce DNS/web filtering against known fake-download and traffic distribution domains.
  • P2Monitor for unauthorized remote access tools such as NetSupport RAT running outside approved IT use.
  • P3Maintain offline, tested backups given the group's ties to destructive ransomware variants that can erase files even after payment.
Analyst Note

FIN7 is one of the longest-running financially motivated hacking groups still active today, and its longevity comes from operating like an actual business rather than a loose crew: separate teams handle development, phishing, and money laundering, so an arrest in one part of the operation rarely shuts down the rest. Organizations should pay attention because the group has consistently stayed ahead of defenses by diversifying, moving from point-of-sale malware to mailed USB drives to fake software downloads to malicious ads, and by selling its own EDR evasion tooling to other criminal groups. This matters because despite multiple US indictments dating back to 2018, credible FIN7-linked infrastructure and campaigns were still being documented as recently as 2026, which suggests the group's core capability and personnel have outlasted any single law enforcement action against it.
