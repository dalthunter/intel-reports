Threat Cluster Profile: Salesforce-Focused Vishing & Data Extortion

UNC6040

Google Threat Intelligence Group designation · extortion handoff observed to UNC6240 / ShinyHunters

A financially motivated cluster tracked by Google since mid-2025, specializing in a single, repeatable move: calling an employee, posing as IT support, and talking them into authorizing a fake Salesforce Data Loader app that quietly exports the entire CRM.

Analyst Note

UNC6040 typically targets organizations by using voice phishing (vishing) to trick employees into giving attackers access to cloud accounts. The attackers can impersonate IT staff or other trusted employees and convince victims to provide information or approve changes to their accounts. Once inside, they can use stolen credentials to access cloud systems, search for sensitive information, and steal data. Their activity focuses heavily on social engineering, identity, and cloud access rather than traditional malware. I would look for unexpected phone-based authentication requests, suspicious password resets, unusual MFA changes, unfamiliar cloud logins, new devices or sessions, abnormal access to cloud data, and large or unusual data downloads.

Section 01

Actor Profile

Origin & designation

UNC6040 is an "uncategorized" cluster designation used by Google Threat Intelligence Group (GTIG) for a set of intrusions it could tie together by shared tradecraft but not yet to a named, persistent group. The cluster's activity was first tied together and disclosed publicly in June 2025, though MITRE ATT&CK's mapping of the associated campaign traces the earliest related intrusions back to October 2024.

Relationship to ShinyHunters & The Com

UNC6040 is the access broker, not the extortionist. Mandiant has been explicit that a separate cluster, tracked as UNC6240 and self-identifying as "ShinyHunters," typically handles the follow-on extortion demand after UNC6040 completes the theft, and the two have been observed operating with no direct evidence of the same individuals behind both. The overlap in targeting Okta credentials and IT support impersonation ties the wider ecosystem to "The Com," the loose network also linked to Scattered Spider, though Google is careful to note that shared tradecraft is not proof of a shared roster.

Targeting philosophy

Unlike broader account-takeover actors seeking network-wide access, UNC6040 has a narrow, CRM-specific objective: get one authorized connected app into a victim's Salesforce environment and export as much customer and contact data as possible before the access is noticed or revoked.

Timeline of Activity

Oct 2024

Campaign onset

MITRE ATT&CK's retrospective mapping places the start of the associated Salesforce Data Exfiltration campaign here, using vishing to compromise corporate Salesforce instances.

Mar 2025

Salesforce customer advisory

Salesforce warns customers directly of threat actors impersonating IT support over the phone to obtain credentials or approval of a modified Data Loader application.

Jun 2025

Google publicly names UNC6040

GTIG publishes "The Cost of a Call," detailing the group's vishing-to-extortion chain against Salesforce customers, alongside disclosure that Google's own corporate Salesforce instance was affected.

Aug 2025

Telegram convergence

A new Telegram channel bringing together ShinyHunters, Scattered Spider, and Lapsus$ personas surfaces, deepening the visible overlap between "The Com" affiliated clusters.

Aug to Sep 2025

UNC6395 / Salesloft Drift incident

A related but distinct cluster abuses stolen OAuth tokens from the Salesloft Drift integration to reach downstream Salesforce environments; the FBI issues a joint advisory covering both UNC6040 and UNC6395.

Sep 2025

Mandiant hardening guidance

Mandiant publishes proactive hardening, logging, and detection recommendations drawn directly from observed UNC6040 tradecraft.

Jan 2026

Related vishing wave, distinct cluster

Mandiant documents a further round of IT-support impersonation vishing against SaaS platforms, attributed to a separate cluster, UNC6661, with no observed overlap in activity with UNC6040.

Section 03

Kill Chain & MITRE ATT&CK Mapping

The entire intrusion can happen without a single line of malware touching the victim's endpoint. The weapon is a phone call and a Salesforce connected app authorization screen.

01

Reconnaissance

Gather Victim Org Information

Operators research a target's org chart and IT support process closely enough to sound credible on a cold call, often using pre-recorded automated phone menus to extract further detail beforehand.

02

Initial Access

T1566.004Spearphishing Voice (Vishing)

A caller impersonating internal IT support convinces an English-speaking employee to navigate to Salesforce's connected-app authorization page.

T1528Steal Application Access Token

The operator pre-configures OAuth Device Flow through a local, actor-controlled Data Loader build and generates a short device code; once the victim enters that code and approves the app, an access token is issued directly to the attacker's instance.

03

Defense Evasion

Disguised Connected Application

The malicious app is styled to resemble Salesforce's legitimate Data Loader tool, so its presence in the connected-apps list does not immediately stand out to an administrator.

Anonymization Infrastructure

Vishing calls have been placed through VPN-anonymized voice services, and follow-on data movement has used Tor, obscuring the operators' true location and call origin.

04

Collection & Exfiltration

T1530Data from Cloud Storage

All subsequent actions run on behalf of the victim's approved session, allowing the attacker's Data Loader instance to pull CRM records in small, slow chunks that resemble routine sync activity rather than a single bulk export.

Lateral Pivot via Compromised Mailbox

In some intrusions, access to a compromised employee mailbox has been reused to send further phishing emails to the victim's own contacts, with the sent messages deleted afterward to cover the trail.

05

Impact

Extortion Handoff

Stolen data is handed off for the extortion phase, typically executed under the separately tracked UNC6240 / ShinyHunters persona, with a rapid payment deadline used to pressure the victim.

Section 05

Detection & Mitigation Priorities

Drawn directly from Mandiant's published UNC6040 hardening guidance, ordered by impact-to-effort ratio.

  • P1Restrict which connected apps employees can self-authorize in Salesforce, and require admin review for anything requesting bulk data export scope.
  • P1Require a live video call to verify identity for any help desk request involving credential reset or new app authorization, not a callback alone.
  • P1Monitor and alert on Data Loader / API-driven bulk exports outside expected integration behavior, especially from new or recently authorized apps.
  • P2Harden and rotate programmatic credentials — API keys, OAuth tokens, service accounts — on a defined schedule rather than indefinitely.
  • P2Enable and centrally log Salesforce Event Monitoring for connected-app authorizations and large report or API exports.
  • P3Pre-brief employees on the specific Data Loader vishing script so a call requesting connected-app approval is treated as suspicious by default.
