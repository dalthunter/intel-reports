Scattered Spider

Section 01

Actor Profile

Origin & structure

Scattered Spider emerged in May 2022, formed largely of native English speaking teenagers and young adults from the US, UK, and Canada, tied to a broader loose network known as "The Com." Members coordinate over Telegram and Discord rather than through a fixed hierarchy, which is part of why the group has kept operating through repeated arrests of individual members.

Targeting philosophy

Early activity in 2022 focused on telecom and business process outsourcing firms; by 2023 the group had expanded into gaming, hospitality, retail, and financial services. Activity often arrives in sector "waves," with weeks of concentrated attacks against one industry, such as retail or insurance, before the group shifts its attention elsewhere.

Extortion model

Data theft and encryption for double extortion pressure, with the group known to move between ransomware brands, including BlackCat/ALPHV, RansomHub, Qilin, and DragonForce, rather than maintaining a single encryptor of its own. Recent activity has also shifted toward encrypting VMware vSphere and ESXi hosts directly, taking every hosted virtual machine down at once.

Section 03

Kill Chain & MITRE ATT&CK Mapping

Scattered Spider's kill chain barely touches malware at the entry point. Almost every intrusion begins with a phone call or a text message rather than an exploit, which makes the group's edge human, not technical.

01

Initial Access

Social Engineering / Help Desk Impersonation

Callers impersonate employees to IT help desks, requesting a password reset or new MFA device enrollment to gain an initial foothold.

SIM Swapping

Operators hijack a target's phone number through telecom social engineering to intercept SMS based MFA codes.

02

Execution & Persistence

Bring Your Own Vulnerable Driver

Signed but vulnerable drivers are installed to disable endpoint security tools at the kernel level.

Valid Accounts / Legitimate Remote Access Tools

Stolen credentials and already trusted tools such as AnyDesk or TeamViewer maintain access without triggering malware alerts.

03

Discovery & Defense Evasion

Cloud Service Discovery

Operators enumerate Okta, Azure AD, and AWS or GCP tenants to map identity providers and privileged accounts before moving further.

MFA Request Generation (Push Bombing)

Repeated MFA push notifications wear down a target until one is approved, or a fresh registration is pushed through the compromised help desk.

04

Collection & Exfiltration

Data from Cloud Storage

SaaS platforms and cloud storage buckets are queried directly for sensitive records ahead of encryption, supporting the double extortion threat.

05

Impact

Data Encrypted for Impact

Once inside virtualization infrastructure, ESXi hosts are encrypted directly at the hypervisor level using an affiliate ransomware brand, taking every hosted virtual machine down in a single action.

Section 05

Detection & Mitigation Priorities

Ordered by impact-to-effort ratio for a mid-sized enterprise.

  • P1Require callback verification through a known number before any help desk password reset or MFA re-enrollment.
  • P1Move to phishing resistant MFA (FIDO2 hardware keys) for privileged and help desk accessible accounts.
  • P1Alert on new MFA device registrations and on repeated push notifications within a short window.
  • P2Restrict and monitor legitimate remote access tools not already approved for the environment.
  • P2Segment and closely monitor administrative access to virtualization platforms such as VMware vSphere and ESXi.
  • P3Prepare an extortion response playbook in advance, including legal, comms, and law enforcement contacts.
Analyst Note

This group mainly gets access through social engineering by tricking employees or help desk staff into resetting passwords or approving new Multi-Factor Authentication (MFA) devices. To stop them, organizations should verify password reset requests, use phishing-resistant MFA, limit help desk account privileges, and train employees to recognize social engineering attacks.
