Threat Actor Profile — Identity-Centric Cybercrime Collective

Scattered Spider

aka UNC3944 · Octo Tempest · Muddled Libra · 0ktapus · Scatter Swine · now allied as "Scattered Lapsus$ Hunters"

A loosely affiliated, largely English-speaking cybercrime collective operating out of "The Com" underground community since 2022, best known for bypassing MFA and technical controls entirely by social-engineering help desks over the phone. Since 2025 the group has rotated methodically through UK/US retail, insurance, aviation, and automotive victims, pairing its long-standing identity-provider tradecraft with DragonForce ransomware for double extortion.

Section 01

Key Judgments

Scattered Spider is not a single fixed group but a fluid membership pool drawn from "The Com," a broader English-speaking cybercrime community. The same operators are tracked under at least six vendor names (Scattered Spider, UNC3944, Octo Tempest, Muddled Libra, 0ktapus, Scatter Swine), and in 2025 the collective began publicly branding jointly with ShinyHunters and Lapsus$ as "Scattered Lapsus$ Hunters" for extortion campaigns.

Initial access is overwhelmingly achieved through voice phishing against IT help desks, not technical exploitation. Operators impersonate employees convincingly enough to talk staff into resetting passwords or registering a new MFA device, per CrowdStrike's direct observation of the group's Q2 2025 incidents. This makes identity verification process, not patching, the group's primary weakness to defend against.

The group has shifted from pure data-theft extortion toward full ransomware deployment through a partnership with the DragonForce RaaS cartel, first confirmed in CISA's July 2025 advisory update. DragonForce payloads target Windows, Linux, and VMware ESXi/Hyper-V infrastructure, shutting down virtual machines ahead of encryption to maximize disruption.

Scattered Spider works in deliberate sector waves rather than opportunistic sprays: UK/US retail (Apr–May 2025: M&S, Co-op, Harrods), US insurance (Jun 2025: Aflac, Erie, Philadelphia Insurance), aviation (Jun–Jul 2025: Hawaiian Airlines, WestJet, Qantas), then automotive/manufacturing (Sep 2025: Jaguar Land Rover, jointly claimed with ShinyHunters). CrowdStrike's May 2026 reporting shows the group resuming ransomware activity against insurance targets after a pause, indicating the sector-rotation model persists.

Arrests have not meaningfully degraded the collective. UK and US authorities charged and arrested individuals tied to the retail wave and the 2024 Transport for London breach in 2025, yet the loosely affiliated, pseudonymous membership model means the brand and tradecraft persist under new participants.

Analyst Note

The throughline across all five judgments is identity, not infrastructure. This group barely needs malware to get in the door — a convincing phone call and a compliant help-desk agent gets them further than most exploit chains would. That reframes the defensive priority: help-desk identity verification and phishing-resistant MFA matter more here than almost any patch cycle.

Section 02

Actor Profile

Origin & structure

Scattered Spider emerged from "The Com," a decentralized online community whose membership skews young — largely teenagers and young adults across the US, UK, and other English-speaking countries. The group first drew attention in mid-2022 running SIM-swap campaigns against telecommunications and business-process-outsourcing (BPO) firms, including a campaign that compromised roughly 125 Twilio customers. Because the same operators surface across multiple vendors' telemetry, the collective is tracked under a confusing tangle of names: CrowdStrike's Scattered Spider, Mandiant's UNC3944, Microsoft's Octo Tempest, Palo Alto's Muddled Libra, Group-IB's 0ktapus, and Okta's Scatter Swine.

Targeting philosophy

Google/Mandiant has described the group's defining trait as sector-focused "waves": Scattered Spider concentrates on one industry for weeks at a time, refining a consistent phishing pretext and social-engineering script before rotating to the next. Confirmed waves include telecom/BPO (2022), gaming and hospitality (2023, including MGM Resorts and Caesars), financial services (late 2023), food service (May 2024), and the 2025 run through UK/US retail, US insurance, aviation, and automotive/manufacturing. ReliaQuest's analysis of 600+ domains linked to the group found roughly 70% of targets fall in technology, finance, and retail trade, with about 81% of the group's phishing domains impersonating technology vendors specifically (keywords like "okta," "helpdesk," "vpn," and "sso" recur heavily in domain registrations).

Extortion model

Early campaigns favored pure data-theft extortion — steal data, threaten to leak it, skip encryption entirely. That has evolved: the group has worked as an affiliate of ALPHV/BlackCat and RansomHub, and since early 2025 has partnered with the DragonForce ransomware cartel for double extortion, encrypting Windows, Linux, and virtualization infrastructure after exfiltrating data to MEGA or attacker-controlled cloud storage. The 2025 "Scattered Lapsus$ Hunters" branding with ShinyHunters and Lapsus$ signals the collective is also converging around large-scale SaaS data-extortion operations, separate from ransomware entirely.

Analyst Note

Treat "Scattered Spider" as a tradecraft signature more than a fixed roster. The individuals cycle, the ransomware partner of the month changes (BlackCat, then RansomHub, then DragonForce), but the help-desk vishing playbook and the sector-wave targeting model have stayed remarkably consistent since 2022. That consistency is what makes this group tractable for detection engineering despite the shifting brand names.

Section 03

Timeline of Activity

Jun 2022

First tracked activity

CrowdStrike begins tracking a campaign targeting telecom and BPO firms for SIM-swap access, including a breach affecting roughly 125 Twilio customers.

Sep 2023

MGM Resorts & Caesars breaches

A help-desk vishing call gets the group into MGM's environment; both MGM and Caesars are hit within weeks, causing major operational disruption and bringing the group to mainstream attention alongside ALPHV/BlackCat ransomware.

2024

Pivot into SaaS and cloud

Mandiant documents the group moving beyond on-prem compromise into direct SaaS/cloud targeting — vCenter, CyberArk, Salesforce, Azure, AWS, and Google Cloud Platform among the applications reached via stolen SSO credentials.

Apr–May 2025

UK retail wave

Help-desk vishing hits Marks & Spencer over the Easter weekend, then Co-op and Harrods in quick succession. M&S loses NTDS.dit domain password hashes and suffers weeks of disrupted online ordering and in-store payments; DragonForce ransomware is confirmed by M&S in UK parliamentary testimony.

Jun–Jul 2025

Insurance and aviation waves

Targeting rotates to US insurers (Aflac, Erie Indemnity, Philadelphia Insurance) then airlines (Hawaiian Airlines, WestJet, and a Qantas third-party contact-center breach). The FBI publicly warns of the aviation-sector shift on June 27, 2025.

Jul 2025

Joint advisory update & UK arrests

CISA, the FBI, NCSC-UK, and Australian and Canadian partners issue an updated joint advisory reflecting FBI investigations through June 2025. The UK's NCA arrests four individuals tied to the retail wave; the combined M&S/Co-op incident is later estimated at £270–440M in impact by the Cyber Monitoring Centre.

Sep 2025

Jaguar Land Rover & "Scattered Lapsus$ Hunters"

JLR halts production after a breach jointly claimed by Scattered Spider and ShinyHunters, reportedly via a flaw in third-party SAP NetWeaver software. The two groups, alongside Lapsus$, begin publicly branding as a single "Scattered Lapsus$ Hunters" collective.

May 2026

Ransomware activity resumes against insurers

CrowdStrike's 2026 Financial Services Threat Landscape Report documents Scattered Spider resuming aggressive ransomware operations against insurance entities after a multi-month pause, returning to one of its historically favored sectors.

Analyst Note

The 2025 wave pattern (retail → insurance → aviation → automotive) reads less like opportunism and more like a playbook being run methodically against one sector's help-desk culture at a time. The May 2026 return to insurance after arrests and a pause is the most useful forward-looking signal here: expect the group to keep resuming prior sectors rather than abandoning them permanently after law enforcement pressure.

Section 05

Kill Chain & MITRE ATT&CK Mapping

Scattered Spider's kill chain is unusual in how little of it depends on malware. The group's edge is entirely in convincing a human being to hand over access, then living off legitimate remote-access tools once inside. MITRE tracks this group as G1015; CISA's AA23-320A advisory (updated through June 2025 FBI investigations) maps the fuller technique set.

01

Initial Access

T1598 / T1656Phishing for Information & Impersonation

Operators call IT help desks directly, impersonating a locked-out employee or executive. CrowdStrike observed the group routinely answering help-desk identity-verification questions correctly, indicating pre-call OSINT against the target employee.

T1621Multi-Factor Authentication Request Generation

Push-bombing (MFA fatigue) is used both directly against victims and to pressure help-desk staff into approving a fraudulent MFA device registration during a live call.

T1078Valid Accounts

SIM-swapping cellular carriers to intercept SMS-based MFA and password-reset codes remains part of the group's toolkit from its earliest telecom-focused campaigns onward, alongside typosquatted SSO/VPN phishing kits.

02

Execution & Persistence

T1219Remote Access Software

Commercial remote monitoring and management tools — AnyDesk, ScreenConnect, TeamViewer, Splashtop, Pulseway, Tactical RMM — are deployed to establish persistence that looks like ordinary IT tooling rather than malware.

T1556.006Modify Authentication Process: MFA

New MFA devices are registered under compromised identities to retain access even after a victim resets the original password, a persistence path that survives routine credential rotation.

T1136Create Account

Federated identity/SSO abuse and new account creation within Entra ID, Okta, and similar identity providers has been used to maintain a foothold that isn't tied to any single compromised user.

03

Privilege Escalation & Defense Evasion

T1562.001Impair Defenses (BYOVD)

The group has used a signed-but-vulnerable driver (dubbed POORTRY) delivered through a loader (STONESTOP) to kill EDR and AV processes from kernel level — technique documented across multiple Scattered Spider-linked BYOVD incidents.

T1036Masquerading

Legitimate-looking tool names and renamed binaries are used throughout to blend RMM and tunneling traffic into normal administrative activity baselines.

T1078.004Valid Accounts: Cloud Accounts

Once inside identity infrastructure, the group pivots directly into SaaS and cloud consoles — vCenter, CyberArk, Salesforce, Azure, AWS, and GCP have all been documented as post-compromise targets, largely without needing further exploitation.

04

Credential Access & Lateral Movement

T1003.006 / T1558DCSync & Credential Dumping

Mimikatz and Impacket's secretsdump are used for credential dumping and DCSync-style domain credential replication once a foothold with sufficient privileges is established.

T1087 / T1482Account & Domain Trust Discovery

SharpHound/BloodHound collection and tools like ADRecon and PingCastle are used to map Active Directory attack paths toward Domain Admin and identify the fastest privilege-escalation route.

T1021Remote Services (RDP/SSH)

Standard RDP and SSH abuse moves the operator laterally once domain credentials are in hand — like The Gentlemen's playbook elsewhere in this portfolio, nothing exotic, which again favors baseline anomaly detection over signature hunting.

05

Command & Control / Exfiltration

T1572Protocol Tunneling

Ngrok, Tailscale, and similar tunneling services proxy C2 and remote-access traffic through infrastructure that looks like ordinary developer or business tooling.

T1567Exfiltration to Cloud Storage

Data is staged and exfiltrated to MEGA or attacker-controlled Amazon S3 buckets ahead of any ransomware deployment, underpinning the double-extortion threat even in intrusions where encryption never occurs.

06

Impact

T1490Inhibit System Recovery

Backup infrastructure and virtualization management (VMware vCenter/ESXi, Hyper-V) are specifically targeted and shut down or destroyed ahead of encryption to remove recovery options and maximize pressure to pay.

T1486Data Encrypted for Impact

Since early 2025, DragonForce ransomware payloads — cross-platform across Windows, Linux, and ESXi — have been deployed following the exfiltration stage, confirmed in M&S's own account of its April 2025 incident.

Analyst Note

Almost the entire kill chain here uses legitimate software or well-worn Windows/AD tooling rather than custom malware. That's genuinely harder to signature than a bespoke ransomware binary, but it also means behavioral and identity-centric detections — verification-call anomalies, new MFA device registrations, RMM tools appearing where they shouldn't be — carry more weight than EDR alone.

A note on tradecraft evolution

What has changed most since 2022 isn't the group's core method — it's the confidence and speed of the social-engineering script. Public reporting from CrowdStrike notes operators now consistently answer help-desk identity-verification questions correctly on the first attempt, indicating pre-call reconnaissance against named employees rather than generic pretexting. The group's willingness to publicly rebrand and merge with ShinyHunters and Lapsus$ in 2025 is also notable; treating "Scattered Lapsus$ Hunters" as a durable, unified extortion brand is a reasonable working assumption going into 2026.

Analyst Note

I'm deliberately keeping the CVE table short and not padding it with tangential vulnerabilities. This group's actual leverage point is a phone call to your help desk, not a patch you're missing — that's the finding worth acting on, even if it's less satisfying than a long exploit list.

Section 08

Detection & Mitigation Priorities

Ordered by estimated impact-to-effort ratio for a mid-sized enterprise defending against this actor's observed playbook.

  • P1Harden help-desk identity verification with call-back to a known-good number, manager attestation, or video verification before any password reset or MFA re-enrollment. This is the group's dominant initial-access vector, and it's a process fix, not a technology purchase.
  • P1Deploy phishing-resistant MFA (FIDO2/WebAuthn or PKI-based) on all SSO, VPN, and privileged accounts. CISA specifically calls this out as resistant to the push-bombing and SIM-swap techniques this group relies on.
  • P1Alert on new MFA device enrollment immediately following a help-desk-initiated password reset — this exact sequence is the group's signature persistence move.
  • P2Restrict and monitor RMM tool usage (AnyDesk, ScreenConnect, TeamViewer, Splashtop, etc.) via allow-listing, since the group relies on these being treated as trusted IT software rather than flagged as remote-access tooling.
  • P2Move backup and virtualization management off the domain and behind dedicated, non-domain credentials. vCenter/ESXi and backup infrastructure are named, specific targets ahead of DragonForce encryption.
  • P2Enable driver-load monitoring / WDAC or HVCI to catch BYOVD attempts (POORTRY/STONESTOP) before EDR is killed at the kernel level.
  • P3Monitor for domain registrations impersonating your own SSO/helpdesk/VPN branding, since ~81% of this group's phishing infrastructure follows that exact pattern.
  • P3Baseline and alert on DCSync-style replication requests and SharpHound/BloodHound-style AD enumeration traffic from non-administrative hosts.
Analyst Note

If you can only act on three items here: fix help-desk identity verification, roll out phishing-resistant MFA, and alert on new-MFA-device-after-reset. Everything else reduces risk meaningfully, but those three directly close the specific path this group has used against every named 2025 victim in this report.
