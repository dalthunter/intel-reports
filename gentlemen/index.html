Section 01

Actor Profile

Origin & structure

The Gentlemen surfaced in mid-2025, founded by an operator known as hastalamuerte (also linked to the alias zeta88) after a public falling-out with the Qilin ransomware-as-a-service program. The group ran as a closed operation through the summer before opening an affiliate program in September 2025, absorbing operators who had previously worked with Embargo, LockBit, Medusa, and BlackLock.

Targeting philosophy

Unlike spray-and-pray ransomware crews, The Gentlemen researches targets before committing resources, favoring organizations whose revenue and cyber-insurance coverage make a paid settlement likely. Initial access runs almost entirely through internet-facing edge devices, chiefly unpatched Fortinet FortiGate appliances, and credentials purchased from infostealer-log markets rather than phishing.

Extortion model

Standard double extortion: data is exfiltrated before encryption, and a leak-site posting backs the ransom demand if the victim refuses to pay. Demands are reportedly calibrated to a victim's likely insurance ceiling using firmographic revenue data, and affiliates keep up to 90–93% of proceeds, an unusually generous split built to pull talent away from rival programs.

Section 03

Kill Chain & MITRE ATT&CK Mapping

The Gentlemen's kill chain leans almost entirely on unpatched edge devices and purchased credentials rather than phishing, then moves fast once inside — affiliates are known to enumerate, evade, and detonate within days of gaining a foothold.

01

Initial Access

Exploit Public-Facing Application

Affiliates target internet-facing Fortinet FortiGate and Cisco appliances, most often via the FortiOS/FortiProxy authentication-bypass flaw CVE-2024-55591, alongside CVE-2025-32433 and CVE-2025-33073.

Valid Accounts

Where an edge device isn't exposed, affiliates authenticate directly using credentials bought from dark-web infostealer-log markets or brute-forced against exposed VPN and RDP endpoints.

02

Execution & Persistence

Command and Scripting Interpreter

PowerShell and native Windows command-line tools disable security controls, enumerate Active Directory, and identify privileged accounts ahead of deployment.

Scheduled Task / Remote Access Software

Persistence is maintained through scheduled tasks and self-restarting services, often alongside a legitimate remote-access tool installed under a disguised name.

03

Discovery & Defense Evasion

Impair Defenses

Operators disable Microsoft Defender, add broad exclusions, delete shadow copies, and strip prefetch data, RDP logs, and PowerShell history to slow incident response.

Exploitation for Privilege Escalation

UAC bypasses and vulnerable-driver abuse elevate a foothold to domain-level access before the encryptor is staged.

04

Collection & Exfiltration

Automated Exfiltration

Sensitive data is staged and pulled out, frequently over an unauthorized Cloudflare Zero Trust tunnel registered inside the victim's own environment, before encryption begins, underpinning the double-extortion threat.

05

Impact

Data Encrypted for Impact

A custom Go-based encryptor using hybrid Curve25519/XChaCha20 encryption self-propagates across the network via multiple simultaneous lateral-movement techniques, typically pushed domain-wide through a modified Group Policy Object.

Section 05

Detection & Mitigation Priorities

Ordered by impact-to-effort ratio for a mid-sized enterprise.

  • P1Patch internet-facing FortiGate and Cisco appliances immediately, prioritizing CVE-2024-55591, CVE-2025-32433, and CVE-2025-33073.
  • P1Disable NTLMv1 and enforce MFA on every VPN and remote-access endpoint.
  • P1Alert on shadow-copy deletion, Defender tampering, and log/prefetch clearing as near-certain precursors to encryption.
  • P2Audit and remove unauthorized Cloudflare Zero Trust tunnels and unexpected remote-access tooling.
  • P2Move backup infrastructure behind dedicated, credential-rotated management VLANs isolated from the production domain.
  • P3Prepare an extortion response playbook in advance, including legal, comms, and law enforcement contacts.
Analyst Note

The Gentlemen is a ransomware-as-a-service operation that broke off on its own after a split from a larger group, and it has grown fast by recruiting outside affiliates rather than relying on one team. They typically get in through internet-facing devices like unpatched Fortinet firewalls or with credentials bought from criminal marketplaces, then quietly map out a network for days before locking everything down and threatening to leak stolen data. Organizations should pay attention because the group is deliberate about picking targets it expects can pay, not opportunistic mass scanning, which means a hit is more likely to become a serious, costly incident. This matters because the fixes are straightforward and well understood appliances and lock down remote access so the same handful of gaps keep getting exploited.
